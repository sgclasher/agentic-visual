CONTEXT

There are many KPIs that can supplement an assessment of business value, and ServiceNow has
developed the AI Agent Value Driver (AAVD) framework to provide guidance on one method that our
customers and partners may be able to use to measure the value of ServiceNow's Agentic AI offerings.
This framework was developed collaboratively across teams of subject matter experts at ServiceNow: AI
Solution Success, Gen AI Lighthouse, Go to Market, Now on Now, Product/Program Management and
Value Excellence. Collective insights from our customers are also embedded in the methodology, as
these cross-functional teams have been working closely with early adopters to measure and validate
business value realized. Internally, AAVD feeds and unifies our value measurement tools across pre- and
post-sale via Value Composer and Now Assist Analytics, respectively. Regardless of whether an
assessment leverages AAVD, we recommend taking a holistic view in collaboration with the customer
whenever possible.

GENERAL APPROACH

Our approach to measuring value aligns to the funnel shown below; the components include:

     A. Addressable workload: we start by asking the question "where do ServiceNow AI Agents come
          into play?" This is a cross-section of ServiceNow's AI Agent capabilities and the customer's
          intended use case(s). As an example, the number of unstructured software provisioning requests
          would provide a top of funnel estimate (addressable workload) for a software request fulfillment
          agent, and could be used to estimate potential value creation for that value driver.

     B. Usage: AI Agents can only `go to work' through adoption, which is often dependent on user
          awareness, proficiency, and trust; deployment decisions such as auto vs. on-demand triggers
          are additional factors. Investments in organizational change management and training play a
          critical role in driving meaningful usage, an early key step in generating value capture.

     C. Usefulness: as we move through the value funnel, we further account for the % of time the AI
          Agent `hits the mark,' since value claims are only valid when the user deems the AI to be useful.
          Criteria is dependent on the AI Agent in use, which we will outline in the following sections. A few
          examples are:
                · Accuracy or relevance of a summarization agent can be assessed through user
                     feedback / survey or similarity scores.
                · Success rates of AI Agent conversations can be assessed through user feedback, user
                     follow-on actions, or pre-determined completion criteria.

                                                                                    2
                · Acceptance rates for ServiceNow objects (e.g., catalog items, flows) generated by AI
                     Agents are typically a straightforward marker of usefulness.

                · Additional considerations: deployment decisions not only affect usage/adoption, but
                     also factor into usefulness ratings; we have observed on-demand usage sometimes
                     producing higher usefulness vs. auto-generated actions that may be ignored by the
                     intended recipient. The takeaway here is that organizational change management
                     continues to play an important role in value realization.

     D. Impact: this represents the unit value of a useful action/use case fulfilled by an AI Agent, which is
          rooted in the counterfactual of `what would have occurred in the absence of the AI Agent?' As
          a quick example - in the SW provisioning automation use case, we would use the amount of
          time it would have taken a human agent to intake, triage, and fulfill the request (e.g., potentially
          30 mins). We could also consider requestor productivity based on administrative time saved and
          reduced user disruption/latency as part of the value equation.

Each value driver in AAVD aligns to specific AI Agent skills1 or Agentic AI workflows2 and conforms to the
approach described above. They are essentially `Lego blocks' of value that contain details on the A, B,
C, and D components of the value funnel, specific to the use case being addressed.

Please note that the value funnel components are used differently in a pre- vs. post-sale setting.
     · Pre-sale, the objective is to evaluate the potential value that a customer can attain from using
          ServiceNow AI Agents. This can be calculated as [A] x [projected capture] x [D]. The [projected
          capture] requires forecasting and validation with the customer, and implicitly includes [B] and
          [C] via assumptions.
     · Post-sale, the objective is to assess value realization `actuals' from ServiceNow AI Agents. This
          can be calculated as [B] x [C] x [D]; furthermore, [A] - [B] provides a perspective into
          uncaptured value potential.

1. `AI Agents' take pointed actions using Now Assist skills, and can be used on-demand by a user
2. `Agentic AI workflows' are executed autonomously by teams of AI Agents managed by an AI Agent orchestrator
to deliver a specific outcome (end-to-end)

AI AGENT VALUE DRIVER DETAILS (applies to ITSM, CSM, HR)

The AI Agent Value Drivers for case management are broken out into three sections: Assistive AI for
Fulfillers, Assistive AI for Requestors, and Autonomous AI Agents.

     · Value drivers under Assistive AI for Fulfillers speak to AI Agent value in the context of fulfiller
          augmentation for pointed tasks/actions (back office).

     · Value drivers under Assistive AI for Requestors represent value for self-service use cases that are
          initiated by an end-user but not managed by an AI Agent orchestrator.

     · Value drivers under Autonomous AI Agents embody the value from workflows orchestrated and
          executed by a team of AI Agents.

Autonomous AI Agents
While Agentic AI workflows are emergent and seemingly more complex, they are just as compatible
with our value measurement approach. What's unique about these value drivers are the expansive
`addressable workload' categories enabled by the AI Agent orchestrator and the correspondingly
higher magnitudes for value impact (end-to-end workflow automation vs. single task completion). OOB
Autonomous AI Agents will be delivered against ServiceNow's roadmap, alongside custom AI Agents
that will be created by partners and customers for white space needs. Initial OOB use cases will include
process orchestration and auto-resolution for simple unstructured requests, and eventually OOB
Autonomous AI Agents will be capable of tackling more complex and ambiguous assignments.

BUSINESS VALIDATION & CONCLUDING THOUGHTS

As a reminder, the value drivers provide a bottoms-up approach to measuring the value of AI Agents.
They offer an activity-based view of value, and it is best supplemented by comparing the aggregation
points to higher level business metrics. For example, with Service Management, the sum of fulfiller time
savings should relate to reduced `cost to serve', or a business KPI such as [#overall employees] : [service
desk / helpdesk employees]. The residual effect of increased self-service/deflection + fulfiller
augmentation should manifest as an improvement in this staffing ratio; in other words, fewer fulfillers
should be required to provide services to the same (or more) end-users.

Similar business KPIs and ratios can be applied to a customer contact center, NOC, SOC, etc. For the
PM and developer org, and employees at large, improvements in productivity should relate to
increased throughput and flow metrics.

Also, keep in mind that value accrues along other business dimensions such as satisfaction, quality, and
risk. Satisfaction can be measured through eSAT (requestor, fulfiller, developer) and cSAT/NPS, while
MTTR also offers a window into experiential value. Quality can be evaluated through reductions in
rework (which can also lead to hard cost take-out) or delivery scope, and risk can be measured
through business KPIs such as uptime, resilience, and risk scores.

AAVD was designed to provide in-platform value measurement by focusing on readily available
customer usage data. Most business KPIs can't be measured through in-platform signals and require
higher levels of customer engagement for value triangulation and validation. Again, there are many
other KPIs that can supplement a customer's assessment of business value, and we recommend taking
a holistic approach in collaboration with the customer whenever possible.
